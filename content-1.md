# **FAQ**

## **Questions Authors Ask Before They Begin**

### **Do I need a finished manuscript to work with Scriptora?**

No. Depending on your project, we can work with a completed manuscript, an early draft, a book concept, or an idea that still needs development. During the initial consultation, we'll determine what stage you're at and what kind of support would be most appropriate.

### **Does Scriptora publish all types of books?**

We work with authors across a broad range of genres, including fiction, nonfiction, memoir, biography, children's books, poetry, inspirational works, business books, educational titles, and more. The exact services and production requirements depend on the book.

### **Do I keep ownership of my book?**

Our assisted self-publishing model is designed around author ownership. Your intellectual property remains yours, subject to the specific terms of your agreement with Scriptora.

### **Can Scriptora help me write my book?**

Yes. If you have an idea, outline, notes, recordings, or an incomplete manuscript, our writing and ghostwriting specialists can help develop the material into a cohesive manuscript while working closely with you on your vision and voice.

### **Can you edit a manuscript I've already written?**

Absolutely. Depending on what your manuscript needs, we can provide developmental editing, line editing, copyediting, proofreading, or a combination of editorial services.

### **Do you provide book cover design and interior formatting?**

Yes. Our book design services can include custom cover design, interior layout, typography, typesetting, and preparation for print and digital formats. Children's and illustrated books can also receive custom illustration support.

### **Can my book be published in print and ebook formats?**

Yes. We can prepare books for professional print and digital publication, with the appropriate formats determined by your project and distribution requirements.

### **Do you help with Amazon and other book retailers?**

We can assist with publishing and distribution setup so your book can be made available through appropriate retail and digital channels. The specific platforms and availability depend on the publishing and distribution arrangement selected for your project.

### **How much does publishing a book cost?**

There is no single price that makes sense for every book. A children's picture book with custom illustrations, a 90,000-word novel, and a professionally developed business book have very different production requirements.

Rather than forcing every author into the same package, we first understand your project and recommend the services that actually fit it.

### **How long does the publishing process take?**

Timelines vary depending on the manuscript's condition, editing requirements, design complexity, illustration requirements, author approvals, and publishing specifications. We'll establish a realistic project timeline before production begins.

### **Do you offer marketing?**

Yes. Marketing support can include author positioning, book marketing assets, launch support, promotional strategy, digital marketing, and audience development. The appropriate approach depends on your book, genre, goals, and target readers.

# **SECTION 9 — FAQ**

**Eyebrow:**  
BEFORE THE FIRST PAGE

**Main Heading:**  
Questions from the story desk.

**Description:**  
Every children's book begins differently. These are some of the questions authors ask us before their project takes shape.

## **I only have an idea. Is that enough to start?**

Absolutely. Some children's books arrive as finished manuscripts; others begin with a character, lesson, bedtime story, rough outline, or a few lines scribbled in a notebook.

We can assess what you have and determine whether the next step should be story development, children's book writing, editing, illustration planning, or production.

## **How do I know what age group my children's book is for?**

We'll consider the concept, word count, vocabulary, narrative complexity, illustration needs, and intended reading experience. These factors help establish whether the project is best suited to a picture book, early reader, illustrated chapter book, or another format.

## **Can you help shorten or edit my children's book?**

Yes. Children's book editing often involves more than correcting grammar. We can work on clarity, word economy, rhythm, repetition, vocabulary, pacing, dialogue, page-turn opportunities, and read-aloud flow while protecting the author's voice.

## **Can I choose the illustration style?**

The visual direction is developed collaboratively. We consider your preferences alongside the story's age group, genre, emotional tone, characters, and intended reading experience. References and character direction are established before final illustration proceeds.

## **Will my characters look consistent throughout the book?**

Character consistency is part of the illustration process. Appearance, proportions, clothing, expressions, recurring details, and environments are considered across the book so the same character doesn't mysteriously become somebody else halfway through the adventure.

## **Will I see the illustrations before the book is finalized?**

Yes. The workflow includes review points for relevant creative stages, which can include character direction, storyboard development, illustrations, cover design, and complete layouts.

## **How many illustrations does my children's book need?**

There isn't one correct number. A 32-page picture book, early reader, activity book, and illustrated chapter book all use imagery differently.

We determine illustration requirements from the manuscript, page plan, format, and visual storytelling needs rather than assigning artwork arbitrarily.

## **Can you create both the cover and interior illustrations?**

Yes. Cover design and interior illustration can be developed as one visual system so the book feels consistent from the moment a reader sees the cover to the final page.

## **Can you prepare my children's book for print and ebook publication?**

Yes. Depending on your project, we can prepare professional print files and suitable digital formats, with layout and production requirements handled according to the publishing route selected.

## **Can you help publish and distribute the finished children's book?**

Yes. Scriptora can support the publishing and distribution stages after production. The exact platforms, formats, and distribution route are determined according to the publishing arrangement chosen for your project.

## **Who owns my story and finished book?**

Ownership, licensing, illustration usage, source-file provisions, and other rights are clearly documented in your individual project agreement before production begins, so you understand the arrangement from the outset.

# **SECTION 8 — FAQ**

**Eyebrow:**  
BOOK PUBLISHING / QUESTIONS

**Main Heading:**  
Understand the process before your book enters it.

**Description:**  
No two publishing projects are identical, but authors tend to arrive with many of the same practical questions. Here are the answers worth knowing before production begins.

## **Does every book need the complete publishing process?**

No.

A manuscript that's already professionally edited and designed may only require production, publication, or distribution support. Another may need substantial editorial development before anyone should think about formatting it.

We review where the book actually stands first, then define the appropriate scope.

## **Can you publish a manuscript that isn't completely finished?**

Potentially, yes—but the right starting point may be manuscript development or editing rather than publication.

Publishing too early can turn unfinished editorial decisions into expensive production changes later. We'll identify the appropriate next stage after reviewing the project.

## **How long does professional book publishing take?**

There isn't one responsible timeline for every book.

Length, manuscript readiness, editorial depth, cover development, interior complexity, illustrations, selected formats, revision rounds, author approvals, and publishing requirements can all affect the schedule.

Your project timeline should reflect the book, not an arbitrary promise.

## **Will I approve my book before it is published?**

Yes.

Relevant approvals are built into the agreed workflow, including major creative stages and final proofs or production files before authorized publication steps.

## **Do I need both a paperback and an ebook?**

Not necessarily.

Format decisions should reflect the book, audience, commercial goals, and reading experience. Some books benefit strongly from both; others may require print-first, digital-first, hardcover, or more specialized production choices.

## **What is an ISBN, and will my book need one?**

An ISBN is an identifier used to distinguish a specific book edition and format within the publishing and bookselling ecosystem.

Whether one is required—and how identifiers should be handled, depends on the formats, publishing arrangement, and distribution route selected for your book.

## **Can you help with Amazon publishing and print-on-demand?**

Where included in the agreed publishing scope, Scriptora can assist with preparing the files, book information, and setup requirements for selected publishing and print-on-demand channels.

The exact platforms and account responsibilities are confirmed for each project.

## **Can my book be available beyond Amazon?**

Yes, depending on the distribution route selected for the project.

Wider availability can involve additional retail, wholesale, library, or digital channels. Distribution strategy should be determined by the book's formats, audience, market, and publishing arrangement rather than by simply listing as many platforms as possible.

## **Do I retain ownership of my book?**

Ownership, intellectual-property rights, licences, source files, publishing-account access, royalties, and usage permissions should be clearly documented in your project agreement.

We believe those details belong in writing, not hidden behind vague website promises.

## **What happens after the book is published?**

Publication makes the book available.

Discovery is a different challenge.

Depending on your goals, the next stage may involve distribution expansion, author positioning, book marketing, launch activity, an author website, audience development, or simply beginning the next book.

# **SECTION 8 — FAQ**

**Eyebrow:**  
GHOSTWRITING / QUESTIONS

**Main Heading:**  
The questions worth asking before someone writes in your voice.

**Description:**  
Ghostwriting is an unusually close creative partnership. Voice, authorship, confidentiality, involvement, research, and rights should be understood before the manuscript starts growing.

## **Will the manuscript actually sound like me?**

That's one of the central measures of successful ghostwriting.

We study your natural vocabulary, cadence, perspective, humor, storytelling habits, existing writing, and feedback. A voice-development stage or sample chapter provides an early opportunity to calibrate the writing before full drafting progresses.

## **If someone else writes it, am I still the author?**

Ghostwriting arrangements are specifically designed around situations where a professional writer helps transform an author's ideas, expertise, experiences, and source material into a manuscript.

However, credit, copyright, confidentiality, acknowledgements, permitted portfolio use, and other authorship matters should always be defined explicitly in your individual agreement rather than assumed.

## **How much of the book needs to come from me?**

Enough to make it genuinely your book.

That may include interviews, memories, expertise, arguments, documents, stories, research, existing writing, feedback, or factual review.

The ghostwriter handles the writing process, but the substance and direction of the book depend on the nature of your project and the source material available.

## **What if I only have an idea?**

That's a valid starting point.

An early-stage project may begin with discovery conversations designed to determine the book's central idea, reader, purpose, scope, potential structure, and what additional material would be needed before drafting.

## **Can you work from recordings, notes, presentations, or existing material?**

Yes, provided the material can legally be used and shared for the project.

Depending on the book, source material might include notes, journals, presentations, articles, interviews, transcripts, recordings, research, speeches, correspondence, previous drafts, or other relevant documents.

## **Will I need to be interviewed?**

For many memoir, business, expertise-led, and thought-leadership projects, interviews are one of the most valuable ways to capture knowledge and natural voice.

The number, length, and structure of conversations depend on the book and the amount of source material already available.

## **What if I'm not good at explaining myself?**

You don't need to arrive speaking in book-ready paragraphs.

A skilled ghostwriting interview isn't a performance. It's a conversation designed to uncover stories, details, explanations, opinions, and language that can later be shaped on the page.

Sometimes the most useful material arrives after someone says, “I'm not sure how to explain this.”

## **Can you ghostwrite fiction?**

Fiction ghostwriting is possible, but it requires a different process from memoir or expertise-led nonfiction.

Plot, character development, world-building, point of view, genre expectations, narrative voice, and creative ownership all need to be established carefully before substantial drafting begins.

## **How long does ghostwriting a book take?**

The timeline depends on the book's length, complexity, research requirements, interview schedule, availability of source material, revision depth, and how quickly approvals and feedback are provided.

A serious manuscript should be scheduled around what the work requires rather than an arbitrary promise.

## **Is ghostwriting confidential?**

Confidentiality requirements should be defined in the project agreement.

That can include how source material is handled, who knows about the collaboration, whether the writer may discuss the project, and whether any portfolio or acknowledgement use is permitted.

## **What happens once the manuscript is finished?**

A completed ghostwritten manuscript can move into the appropriate editorial and publishing stages.

Depending on the agreed scope, that may include final editing, proofreading, cover design, formatting, print and ebook preparation, publishing, distribution, and marketing.

# **SECTION 3 — THE FOUR EDITORIAL LAYERS**

**Section Label:**  
THE CRAFT BEHIND BOOK EDITING

## **01 — DEVELOPMENTAL EDITING**

**Heading:**  
First, make sure the book works.

**Description:**  
Developmental editing looks beyond individual sentences to the architecture holding the manuscript together.

For fiction, that can mean plot, pacing, character motivation, point of view, stakes, scene progression, world-building, and narrative payoff.

For nonfiction, it can mean argument, chapter logic, reader progression, repetition, evidence, positioning, and whether the book actually delivers what it promises.

Before polishing the language, we ask whether the book underneath it is doing its job.

## **02 — LINE EDITING**

**Heading:**  
Then listen to how the writing moves.

**Description:**  
Line editing works where craft becomes voice.

Sentence rhythm. Word choice. Repetition. Transitions. Clarity. Emphasis. Tone. Awkward phrasing. Paragraph movement.

The editor works closely with the prose, not to replace its personality, but to remove the moments that keep the reader from experiencing it.

## **03 — COPYEDITING**

**Heading:**  
Make consistency invisible.

**Description:**  
Names shouldn't change spelling halfway through the book.

A character's eyes shouldn't change color unintentionally.

Capitalization, punctuation, dates, terminology, hyphenation, numbers, citations, and recurring stylistic choices shouldn't depend on which chapter the reader happens to be in.

Copyediting creates that consistency while correcting grammar, usage, mechanics, and appropriate factual or continuity concerns.

Readers rarely notice when it's done well.

They notice when it isn't.

## **04 — PROOFREADING**

**Heading:**  
Catch what survived everything else.

**Description:**  
Proofreading is the last editorial defense before publication.

At this stage, the question is no longer whether chapter seven belongs before chapter six.

It's whether a word disappeared during revision. Whether quotation marks match. Whether a heading is inconsistent. Whether a page break created an awkward result. Whether a caption, running head, folio, or line of text shifted during formatting.

Small errors.

Very visible consequences.

# **SECTION 8 — FAQ**

**Eyebrow:**  
EDITING & PROOFREADING / QUESTIONS

**Main Heading:**  
Know what kind of editing you're paying for.

**Description:**  
“Editing” can mean very different things depending on who is using the word. Understanding those differences helps you commission the right work at the right stage.

## **What's the difference between developmental editing, line editing, copyediting, and proofreading?**

Developmental editing addresses the book at the structural level, plot, character, pacing, organization, argument, reader progression, and other large-scale concerns.

Line editing focuses more closely on the prose itself: clarity, rhythm, word choice, tone, repetition, transitions, and sentence craft.

Copyediting addresses correctness and consistency, including grammar, punctuation, spelling, usage, terminology, continuity, and style decisions.

Proofreading is the final quality-control stage, usually performed after formatting, to catch remaining textual and page-level errors.

## **Which type of editing does my manuscript need?**

That depends on what has already happened to the manuscript and what problems remain.

A sample and project review can help determine whether the priority is structural work, prose refinement, copy consistency, final proofreading, or more than one editorial stage in sequence.

## **Will an editor change my writing style?**

A good editor shouldn't automatically make your writing resemble theirs.

Editorial changes should improve the manuscript while respecting intentional voice, genre, audience, characterization, tone, and stylistic choices.

Where meaningful intervention is needed, tracked changes, comments, queries, or editorial discussion allow you to see and evaluate the reasoning.

## **Will I be able to see the edits?**

For manuscript stages where tracked editing is appropriate, changes and comments can be made visible for author review.

The precise workflow and deliverables are established within the project scope.

## **Do you edit fiction and nonfiction?**

Yes.

The editorial approach should change according to genre and purpose. A fantasy novel, memoir, business book, children's manuscript, and research-led nonfiction project present very different editorial questions.

The editor and scope should reflect those differences.

## **Do I need developmental editing if I've already revised the book several times?**

Not necessarily.

Multiple revisions don't automatically mean the structure is finished, but they don't automatically mean developmental editing is required either.

The manuscript should be assessed based on its current condition rather than the number of drafts behind it.

## **Can you edit a manuscript that has already been edited by someone else?**

Yes.

Previous editing can be useful context, particularly if editorial reports, style sheets, or unresolved queries are available. We assess what the manuscript needs now rather than repeating work simply because it's part of a standard package.

## **Should proofreading happen before or after formatting?**

Final proofreading is generally most valuable after the book has been formatted.

At that point, the proofreader can catch remaining textual mistakes as well as issues visible only in the designed pages, such as awkward breaks, spacing, running heads, captions, page references, and layout-related errors.

## **Can proofreading replace copyediting?**

Usually not.

Proofreading is designed to catch remaining errors late in production, not perform substantial sentence-level correction or establish consistency across an unedited manuscript.

If significant copyediting issues remain, they are better resolved before final layout.

## **How long does editing take?**

The timeline depends on manuscript length, editorial depth, genre, complexity, manuscript condition, author-review stages, and whether multiple editorial passes are required.

A developmental edit of a complex manuscript and a proofread of a polished book are fundamentally different assignments, so the schedule should reflect the actual work.

## **What happens after editing is complete?**

That depends on the manuscript's stage.

A developmental edit may lead into author revision and later line or copyediting. A clean copyedited manuscript may move into book formatting and design. A completed proof may be ready for final production and publishing.
